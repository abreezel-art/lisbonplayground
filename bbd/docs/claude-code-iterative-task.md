# Claude Code Task — Iterative Landing Page Development

## Project Context

You are working on the existing landing page for **Being Before Doing**, a two-day experiential clowning workshop by Pedro Fabião.

This is **not** a redesign from scratch.

This is a careful evolution of an existing website.

The project has already gone through multiple iterations, and many important copy and design decisions have already been made.

Your role is to continue this work carefully, preserving approved decisions while improving weak or unfinished parts.

The project now also contains a structured **Pedro Second Brain** created from workshop transcripts. Use it as a research and writing resource whenever you work on copy, messaging, Pedro’s philosophy, his teaching method, or the workshop experience.

# Source Priority

Use this priority order:

1. **Approved decisions and protected copy** — `copy-status.md`
2. **Page strategy, section goals, and emotional journey** — `creative-brief.md`
3. **Pedro’s original writing** — `pedro-texts.md`
4. **Verified transcript passages** in the Second Brain source folders
5. **Structured Second Brain material** — concepts, vocabulary, metaphors, models, teaching principles, and copywriting directions
6. **Editorial bridge copy** written in a style compatible with Pedro

Important:

- The Second Brain expands the available source material. It does not override approved decisions.
- Pedro’s original writing and verified transcript language take precedence over invented AI copy.
- `pedro-source-material.md` is a compact index, not a replacement for original texts or the Second Brain.
- Direct quotations must be verified against an original source or transcript.
- Do not attribute participant language to Pedro.
- If sources conflict, explain the conflict and ask before changing strategically important or approved copy.

- Do NOT redesign the entire landing page.

- Every significant change must be reviewed and approved before implementation.
- Search Pedro’s existing language before inventing wording.
- Use the Second Brain to deepen weak sections, not to disturb strong approved ones.

# Working Process

For every section, follow exactly this workflow.

## Step 1 — Analyse

Read:

- the existing implementation;
- the relevant part of `creative-brief.md`;
- `copy-status.md`;
- relevant original passages in `pedro-texts.md`;
- relevant themes in the Pedro Second Brain;
- relevant source transcript passages when exact wording matters.

Understand:

- the section’s purpose;
- the visitor’s question;
- what is already working;
- what is approved;
- what is weak, redundant, unsupported, or unclear.

Do NOT modify the code yet.

## Step 2 — Research Pedro’s language

Before drafting, create a compact internal source set:

- 2–5 relevant Pedro quotes or transcript passages;
- 2–4 core concepts;
- 1–3 mental models or distinctions;
- relevant teaching principles;
- cautions against overclaiming.

Search the relevant Second Brain themes as described in `knowledge-base-guide.md`.

Do not paste this research mechanically into the page.

## Step 3 — Present a Proposal

Briefly explain:

### Goal

What this section should achieve.

### What currently works

What should be preserved.

### Problems

What is weak, unclear, repetitive, unsupported, or off-voice.

### Pedro Second Brain material used

Name the themes, concepts, models, or verified phrases that informed the proposal.

### Proposed changes

Exactly what you want to change.

### Why

Explain the reasoning behind your proposal.

### Draft copy, when relevant

Clearly distinguish:

- direct Pedro quotes;
- close adaptations;
- editorial synthesis.

Do NOT implement anything yet.

## Step 4 — Wait

Wait for explicit approval.

We may iterate on the proposal several times.

Do not continue without approval.

## Step 5 — Implement

Only after approval:

- update the code;
- improve layout;
- improve typography;
- improve spacing;
- improve copy where approved.

Keep changes limited to the current section.

Do not silently change neighbouring sections.

## Step 6 — Report and Stop

After implementation, state:

- what changed;
- which files changed;
- which Second Brain material informed the copy;
- whether any direct quote was used and verified;
- any unresolved issue.

Do not continue to the next section automatically.

Wait for feedback.

# Copy Principles

Use Pedro’s own language, concepts, distinctions, and metaphors whenever they genuinely improve clarity.

Before inventing copy:

1. check `pedro-texts.md`;
2. search the relevant Second Brain themes;
3. verify direct quotations in source transcripts.

Use the knowledge base for:

- accurate explanations;
- recurring vocabulary;
- mental models;
- metaphors;
- pedagogical detail;
- section arguments;
- FAQ answers;
- Hero or CTA directions when those areas are open for revision.

Avoid:

- startup language;
- coaching clichés;
- exaggerated promises;
- “transform your life”;
- “unlock your potential”;
- generic self-development marketing;
- therapeutic or healing claims;
- guaranteed catharsis or transformation;
- forcing Pedro’s language into every sentence;
- building a page that reads like a transcript collage.

When connecting paragraphs are needed, write in a style compatible with Pedro:

- embodied;
- precise;
- invitational;
- playful;
- emotionally honest;
- intelligent;
- human.

---

# Knowledge Base Rules

Follow `knowledge-base-guide.md`.

In particular:

- use indexes to locate relevant themes quickly;
- use thematic files to understand concepts and language;
- use source transcripts to verify direct quotes;
- treat mental models marked as editorial as interpretations, not Pedro’s exact diagrams;
- do not quote an unverified “copywriting nugget” as if Pedro said it verbatim;
- do not attribute participant observations to Pedro;
- preserve consent, boundaries, and the validity of a real no;
- do not imply the workshop forces disclosure, performance, catharsis, or emotional exposure.

---

# Design Principles

The page should feel:

- editorial;
- spacious;
- warm;
- artistic;
- intelligent;
- human.

Avoid:

- SaaS aesthetics;
- corporate design language;
- startup UI patterns;
- psychology-sales-page clichés;
- unnecessary animations;
- over-designed diagrams that trivialise Pedro’s ideas.

Mental models from the knowledge base may inspire visual explanations, but they should remain simple, legible, and consistent with the existing editorial direction.

# Preserve Good Work

If an existing section already works well, leave it alone.

Do not change something simply because the Second Brain now offers more material.

Prefer improving weak parts over rewriting strong ones.

The goal is **not** to maximise change.

The goal is to maximise clarity, coherence, specificity, and quality.

---

# When in Doubt

If something is unclear, ask.

Do not guess about:

- approved copy;
- factual claims;
- direct quotations;
- Pedro’s credentials;
- workshop boundaries;
- practical information.

It is better to pause and ask one focused question than to overwrite an approved or source-grounded decision.
