# Why This Workshop Proposal

## 1. Purpose

This section should create recognition before explanation. The visitor should see familiar patterns of over-control, self-editing, fear of mistakes, and disconnection, then feel invited rather than diagnosed or promised a transformation.

## 2. What Works

Preserve:

- The section's position immediately after the Hero.
- The question-led opening: "Do you ever feel like you're trying too hard to get things right?"
- The two-column structure.
- The concise, spacious tone.
- The closing line, which matches the approved brief exactly:

> Being Before Doing is an invitation to experience something different.

Current implementation: `site/Index.html`, section `#why-this-workshop`.

## 3. What Is Weak or Missing

The current copy is clear but compressed:

- "Thinking, controlling" is broad and slightly generic.
- It does not clearly name self-editing or trying to appear "good" or "correct."
- It mentions mistakes but not the fear of what mistakes might mean about belonging or worth.
- It does not include holding emotions privately or losing contact with the body.
- "Connect naturally" is somewhat generic and does not show connection as reciprocal contact.
- The section risks overlapping with the later "About the workshop" copy because both describe stopping performance and becoming more authentic.

## 4. Relevant Pedro Material

Core concepts:

- An overdeveloped "rider": constant thinking, planning, measuring, and delaying action.
- The undertrained "horse": spontaneity, impulse, body, emotion, and aliveness.
- The social mask is real, but incomplete.
- The reaction after a mistake can reveal something personal and alive.
- Mistakes can become material rather than proof of failure.
- Emotion is embodied and becomes meaningful when it reaches another person.

Useful verified transcript passages:

- "We're always thinking and planning and trying to measure... We first think three or four times before we do."
- "The mask is the part of you that you normally use... but it's the one you use to show yourself to the world."
- "The way you react to a mistake is where your clown is living fully."
- "The more you inhabit your body... the more your body starts being filled with your own vital energy."

These are research references only, not proposed website quotations.

## 5. Exact Proposed Copy

Keep the kicker unchanged:

> Why this workshop

Keep the heading unchanged:

> Do you ever feel like you're trying too hard to get things right?

Replace the two paragraphs with:

> Many of us become very good at thinking, planning and measuring ourselves. We worry about how we're perceived, hold back our impulses and keep difficult feelings private.

> We try to stay in control, to be good, correct or easy to be with. We become afraid of mistakes, lose touch with our bodies, and find it harder to play, trust what is happening and create genuine connection.

Keep the closing unchanged:

> Being Before Doing is an invitation to experience something different.

This is editorial synthesis based on Pedro's language, not verbatim copy.

## 6. Design and Layout

Keep the existing overall layout. Proposed refinements:

- Keep the two-column arrangement on desktop.
- Allow the paragraphs to stack earlier on smaller screens, preserving comfortable reading width.
- Give the section slightly more vertical breathing room between the heading and body copy.
- Keep the closing line visually distinct in Cormorant Garamond and gold.
- Do not add cards, icons, illustrations, or a new visual concept.
- Do not alter the Hero or the following Key Themes section.

## 7. Why These Changes

- The first paragraph makes over-control concrete through "thinking, planning and measuring ourselves."
- "How we're perceived" preserves the existing social-recognition theme.
- "Hold difficult feelings private" adds emotional disconnection without making a therapeutic claim.
- "Good, correct or easy to be with" captures the pressure to perform acceptability.
- "Lose touch with our bodies" connects the problem to Pedro's embodied method.
- "Genuine connection" remains invitational while avoiding a guaranteed outcome.
- The copy stays focused on recognition; it does not explain clowning or the workshop method prematurely.
- The unchanged closing preserves the approved strategic direction.
