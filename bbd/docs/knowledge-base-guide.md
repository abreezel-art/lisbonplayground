# Pedro Second Brain — Knowledge Base Guide for Claude Code

## Purpose

The Pedro Second Brain is a structured knowledge base extracted from transcripts of Pedro Fabião’s workshops.

It exists to help you:

- understand Pedro’s philosophy and teaching method more deeply;
- find his real vocabulary, metaphors, distinctions, and recurring ideas;
- discover stronger formulations before inventing new copy;
- explain Being Before Doing accurately;
- create website copy that sounds specific to Pedro rather than like generic self-development marketing;
- trace important ideas back to transcript sources.

It is a research and writing resource, not an automatic replacement for approved website copy.

# Mandatory workflow for copy tasks

## 1. Identify the section’s job

Read:

- `copy-status.md`
- the relevant part of `creative-brief.md`
- the existing implementation

Determine:

- what is already approved;
- what the visitor needs to understand at this point;
- what should remain unchanged;
- what wording is genuinely weak or missing.

## 2. Search the Second Brain before drafting

Use the most relevant thematic files.

Examples:

- **Why This Workshop**  
  Search Core Philosophy, Mistakes, Authenticity, Emotional Work, and Mental Models.

- **State of Play / Why Clowning**  
  Search State of Play, Core Philosophy, Mistakes, and Mental Models.

- **Presence · Authenticity · Connection**  
  Search the three matching themes plus Emotional Work and Connection models.

- **How We Work**  
  Search How Pedro Teaches, State of Play, Presence, Mistakes, and Emotional Work.

- **What Can Be Expected**  
  Search Pedro’s original texts first, then use the Second Brain to clarify and organise.

- **Meet Pedro**  
  Search How Pedro Teaches, Core Philosophy, and examples of his feedback and pedagogy.

- **FAQ**  
  Search Copywriting Nuggets, How Pedro Teaches, Emotional Work, Mistakes, consent/no, and source transcripts.

## 3. Build a small source set

Before proposing new copy, collect a compact internal source set:

- 2–5 relevant Pedro quotes or transcript passages;
- 2–4 recurring concepts;
- 1–3 mental models or distinctions;
- relevant teaching principles;
- any caution that prevents overclaiming.

Do not paste the whole source set into the page. Use it to write grounded, coherent copy.

## 4. Draft in layers

Distinguish internally between:

- **Verbatim Pedro quote**
- **Close adaptation of Pedro’s wording**
- **Editorial synthesis based on the knowledge base**
- **Functional UI copy**

Do not present an adaptation as a direct quote.

## 5. Verify direct quotations

For any text shown as a direct Pedro quote:

- check the original transcript or verified source;
- preserve the meaning and wording;
- do not attribute a participant’s words to Pedro;
- keep a timestamp or source reference in your work notes when available.

If verification is impossible, use the idea as an adaptation without quotation marks.

## 6. Report source use

When presenting a proposal or completing an approved implementation, briefly state:

- which Second Brain themes or files informed the work;
- which Pedro concepts or mental models were used;
- whether any direct quotations were added;
- whether any claim still needs verification.

# What to extract from the knowledge base

## Vocabulary

Prefer Pedro’s recurring words when they fit naturally:

- presence
- connection
- play
- authenticity
- breath
- body
- impulse
- curiosity
- awareness
- humour
- mistakes
- flow
- vulnerability
- sensitivity
- freedom
- experience
- discovery
- attention
- audience
- risk
- emotional expression
- state of play

Do not force vocabulary mechanically.

## Distinctions

Pedro’s voice often becomes clear through distinctions such as:

- being versus doing;
- play versus correct performance;
- authentic reaction versus performed reaction;
- feeling versus showing;
- private emotional process versus shared expression;
- comfort versus safety;
- challenge stress versus threat stress;
- mistake as identity versus mistake as information;
- audience as judge versus audience as partner;
- technique used to reveal versus technique used to hide;
- a real no versus a performed yes;
- trying to be funny versus becoming available to what is funny.

Use these distinctions to make the copy precise.

## Mental models

Use supported models to structure explanations and visual sections.

Examples:

- Play → lower defences → curiosity → experimentation → discovery
- Mistake → honest reaction → amplification → humour / clown
- Attention → body sensation → breath → expression → connection
- Look → ask → receive feedback → adjust
- No → information → new direction
- Trust → faith → leap → discovery
- Personal limit → crossing → transformation
- Small signal → audience response → safety → more expression
- Sensory facts → grounding → presence
- Private emotion → visible expression → relational contact

Label editorial models in internal notes. Do not imply Pedro used an exact diagram unless the source supports it.

## Metaphors and memorable language

The base may include strong images such as:

- the audience builds half the bridge;
- every clown brings a whole new planet;
- jumping without knowing whether there is water in the pool;
- breaking the glass ceiling;
- the abyss of disbelief;
- keeping the antennas open;
- bringing everything into the light;
- a signal that needs to arrive;
- landing in the physical room.

Use them selectively. Do not overload one section with metaphors.

# Writing rules

## Do

- preserve Pedro’s philosophical specificity;
- use concrete, embodied language;
- prefer experience, invitation, and exploration over promises;
- write for non-performers as well as artists;
- connect clowning to life without making inflated claims;
- explain why the audience, body, breath, mistakes, and emotions matter;
- preserve humour and irreverence where appropriate;
- use the knowledge base to deepen weak sections;
- keep approved strong copy intact.

## Do not

- turn Pedro’s work into generic coaching language;
- write “unlock your potential,” “be your best self,” or “transform your life”;
- promise healing, therapy, catharsis, or guaranteed transformation;
- imply people will be forced to disclose or perform;
- erase the importance of consent, boundaries, and a real no;
- quote unverified language;
- attribute participant language to Pedro;
- use transcript grammar errors merely because they are verbatim;
- assemble the page entirely from quotations;
- rewrite a strong approved section only because more material now exists.

# Section-specific use

## Hero

The Hero is protected unless `copy-status.md` says otherwise.

Do not rewrite it merely because the knowledge base contains new phrases.

## Why This Workshop

Use the knowledge base to describe recognisable patterns:

- overthinking;
- self-editing;
- fear of mistakes;
- trying to be good or correct;
- holding emotion privately;
- losing spontaneity;
- difficulty being seen;
- disconnection from body or others.

Do not diagnose the visitor.

## State of Play / Why Clowning

Explain play as a method:

- lowers defence;
- makes experimentation possible;
- changes the relationship to mistakes;
- creates curiosity and pleasure;
- opens presence, authenticity, and connection.

## Presence · Authenticity · Connection

Keep these distinct:

- **Presence:** attention, body, breath, gaze, now.
- **Authenticity:** honest impulse, less self-editing, fuller range.
- **Connection:** expression reaching another person and receiving response.

Avoid three vague cards that say nearly the same thing.

## What Can Be Expected

Begin with `pedro-texts.md`.

Use the Second Brain to:

- clarify headings;
- organise recurring themes;
- remove redundancy;
- explain terms;
- preserve Pedro’s original meaning.

## How We Work

Use actual pedagogy from `How Pedro Teaches`:

- simple rules;
- added constraints;
- games;
- movement;
- partner and group work;
- audience feedback;
- precise observation;
- emotional expression;
- reflection;
- gradual increases in challenge;
- respect for boundaries.

## Meet Pedro

Describe Pedro through:

- what he notices;
- how he creates a field of play;
- how he combines humour, precision, challenge, and support;
- how he uses the audience;
- how he works with mistakes and emotion;
- how he avoids positioning himself as an unquestionable master.

Use CV facts only when supported and necessary.

## FAQ

Base answers on source material.

Important topics may include:

- acting experience;
- need to be funny;
- emotional intensity;
- participation and boundaries;
- being seen by the group;
- difference from therapy;
- workshop methods;
- relevance outside performance.

# Final check before proposing copy

Ask:

- Did I search the Second Brain before inventing wording?
- Is this supported by Pedro’s language or teaching?
- Am I preserving approved decisions?
- Is any quote verified?
- Is the copy coherent rather than a collage?
- Does it sound like Pedro rather than a coaching template?
- Have I avoided promises the material does not support?
- Is the role of play, body, emotion, mistakes, and audience accurately represented?
- Have I respected consent and the possibility of a real no?
