# Section 7: Is This For You? — Proposal

**Status:** Analysis and proposal — not yet implemented  
**Date:** 2026-08-20

---

## Purpose

Help different visitors recognize a valid entry point into the workshop without implying that it is only for performers or professional clowns.

The section should:
- Communicate that the workshop may be relevant to people interested in presence, spontaneity, authentic expression, relational awareness, creativity, and responding rather than performing
- Help each visitor answer: "Could someone like me belong in this workshop, and why?"
- Welcome people with no acting or clowning experience
- Include (where appropriate) the principle: "Lead through presence, not performance"

---

## What Currently Works

**Current website status:** This section does not exist.

**Relevant existing content:**
- Hero page mentions "presence, authenticity and human connection"
- "Why This Workshop" section addresses psychological problems (overthinking, worry, self-control, fear of mistakes)
- "What Can Be Expected" describes outcomes (deep contact, embodied emotional work, play and creativity)

**What is working:**
- The hero and early sections establish emotional need (people who feel controlled and disconnected)
- Testimonials show diverse voices
- The overall page tone suggests this is for "right people," not a generic workshop

**What is missing:**
- No section that explicitly addresses: "This workshop is for you if you are [specific role or interest]"
- No recognition of different entry points (artists, leaders, therapists, coaches, educators, curious humans)
- No clarity about how the workshop applies differently to different people
- No explicit statement about "lead through presence, not performance" or equivalent idea

---

## Problems and Gaps

### 1. Visitor self-recognition is unclear

After reading the current page, a visitor might think:
- "I'm a performer, so maybe this is for me" (but they might not be the primary audience)
- "I'm a leader" (but the page doesn't explain how clowning helps leadership)
- "I'm a therapist" (but is this therapeutic or artistic?)
- "I'm just curious" (am I welcome?)

### 2. No clear positioning for different professions

The creative brief lists:
- Artists
- Leaders
- Therapists
- Coaches
- Educators
- Curious humans

But the website doesn't distinguish *why* each group would benefit. Are the benefits the same for everyone? Or are there specific applications for each?

### 3. "Lead through presence, not performance" positioning is missing

This is a powerful principle from the creative brief. The current page doesn't emphasize this distinction, which may be especially important for leaders (who may fear being asked to become performers).

### 4. The page lacks a "permission structure"

Visitors benefit from explicit permission: "You don't need to have done this before. You don't need to be a performer. This workshop is designed for people who..."

## Recommended Audience Structure

### Option 1: Profession-based entry points (current creative brief model)

**Pros:**
- Specific and clear
- Allows tailored language for each role
- Directly addresses the visitor's identity

**Cons:**
- Risk of generic benefits stated for each profession
- May feel like a menu rather than a coherent whole
- Difficult to avoid repetition

### Option 2: Problem/interest-based entry points

Structure around what the visitor is looking for:
- Greater presence and authentic expression
- Spontaneity and creative risk-taking
- Relational awareness and connection
- Building resilience through playfulness
- Learning to respond rather than perform

**Pros:**
- Unified by theme rather than profession
- Allows cross-category readers to find themselves
- Avoids false segmentation

**Cons:**
- Less immediately identifiable for some visitors
- Requires visitors to self-categorize by interest rather than profession

### Option 3: Hybrid approach (Recommended)

Lead with a strong unified statement about "lead through presence, not performance," then show 3-4 examples of who this applies to, organized by professional role but with connected language.

This preserves professional recognition while maintaining a coherent message.

## Exact Proposed Website Copy

### Opening statement (new section header + intro)

> **Is This For You?**

> This workshop is for anyone whose work or life depends on presence, responsiveness, and connection. Whether you create, lead, teach, heal, or simply want to feel more alive in your relationships, the principle is the same:
>
> **Lead through presence, not performance.**

**Why this wording:**
- "Lead" applies to artists, leaders, teachers, therapists, and coaches
- "Presence, responsiveness, and connection" are universal values
- Establishes a through-line that unifies all audience categories
- The highlighted line is a memorable distinction

### Entry point examples (select 4-5)

> **If you create art or performance**
>
> You are already exploring presence and authenticity. This workshop deepens that work at the level of impulse and real-time reaction, before the mind edits. Many artists find that permission to be imperfect and visible actually increases both freedom and craft.

**Why this wording:**
- Validates existing practice ("already exploring")
- Explains the specific value ("deepens that work")
- Addresses a possible fear ("permission to be imperfect")

---

> **If you lead a team, organization or creative project**
>
> Your presence shapes the room. The workshop trains you to notice your actual effect in real time, respond to what is really happening rather than what you expected, and hold both strength and flexibility. Clown presence—fully embodied, fully present, fully human—builds trust far more than polish.

**Why this wording:**
- Connects to leadership concerns (presence, effect, responsiveness)
- Introduces clown presence as leadership asset, not performance
- Emphasizes the "fully human" principle
- Avoids the trap of promising better business results

---

> **If you work in therapy, coaching or education**
>
> Your effectiveness depends on attunement and the capacity to stay present with what is actually happening. This workshop is not therapy, but it exercises the embodied presence, emotional availability, and authentic contact that underpin all relational work. You will also discover how play and imperfection model for others what it looks like to remain visible during uncertainty.

**Why this wording:**
- Directly addresses the question: "Is this therapeutic?" (No, but...)
- Validates their existing skillset
- Frames the workshop as skill-building, not treatment
- Distinguishes between personal experience and professional practice

---

> **If you are an artist who also acts**
>
> You already understand the power of presence over technique. This workshop deepens your capacity to stay embodied and authentic even under attention. Many performers find that clown work—with its emphasis on real reaction over character—actually strengthens their capacity to be genuinely present in any performance medium.

**Why this wording:**
- Acknowledges their dual identity
- Connects presence to performance quality
- Frames clown work as complementary, not replacement

---

> **If you are simply curious about how you show up in the world**
>
> You don't need a particular profession or artistic practice to belong here. The workshop invites anyone interested in feeling more alive, more spontaneous, and more connected to explore what happens when control loosens and the body gets more of a say. Curiosity is enough.

**Why this wording:**
- Explicitly welcomes the "curious human" category
- Validates curiosity as a sufficient reason
- Sets a warm, inclusive tone
- Avoids gatekeeping

---

### Closing statement (if section is long)

> All of these entry points share a common direction: **away from performing and toward being**. Away from managing how you appear and toward noticing how you actually are. Away from having all the answers and toward discovering what is alive in the present moment—and in the people you meet.

**Why this wording:**
- Brings the audience categories back to a unified principle
- Uses the language from the hero section ("being before doing")
- Emphasizes relationship and presence
- Ends with an invitation rather than a promise

## Layout Recommendations

**Suggested structure:**

1. **Section header + opening paragraph** (unified principle)
2. **4-5 entry point cards or text blocks** (each 3-4 sentences)
   - Minimal visual treatment (light background box, no icons)
   - Left-aligned text, consistent with page style
   - Can be single column or 2-column grid depending on space
3. **Optional: closing statement** (if the section feels too segmented)

**Visual approach:**
- No photographs needed for this section
- Maintain the page's spacious, editorial aesthetic
- Use typography hierarchy to distinguish entry points
- Avoid making it feel like a "corporate audience matrix"

---

## Reasoning

1. **Unified principle first:** The "lead through presence, not performance" idea is more memorable and more useful than a list of professions. It orients the entire section.

2. **Professional specificity second:** After establishing the principle, specific entry points help each visitor recognize themselves without feeling like they're in the wrong audience.

3. **No false segmentation:** Each entry point shares language and values. The benefits are not separate; they are variations on the same theme.

4. **Permission structure:** Explicit statements ("You don't need a particular profession," "Curiosity is enough") reduce barrier to entry.

5. **Honest about what this is:** Clear distinctions (not therapy, not character acting, not a business course) help visitors make informed decisions.

6. **Mirrors the creative brief:** Includes all 6 categories from the brief while organizing them thematically.

## Unresolved Questions

### 1. Should this section come before or after "Meet Pedro"?

**Current proposal placement:** Section 7, between "How We Work" and the Gallery  
**Alternative placement:** After "Meet Pedro," so visitors first understand the facilitator before self-assessing fit  
**Recommendation:** Current placement (7) is better. Visitors self-identify as audience first, then want to know who will facilitate.

### 2. Should the entry points be cards or text blocks?

**Recommendation:** Text blocks with subtle background distinction. Cards may feel too corporate for this page's aesthetic.

### 3. Should we include a fourth or fifth entry point?

Current proposal includes 5. The creative brief lists 6:
- Artists ✓
- Leaders ✓
- Therapists ✓
- Coaches (grouped with therapists: "therapy, coaching, education")
- Educators (grouped with therapists)
- Curious humans ✓

**Recommendation:** Current grouping works. If coaches and educators are distinct professions, those two could be separated, or "coaches" could be in the therapist/coaching/education section with clarification.

### 4. How prominent should "Lead through presence, not performance" be?

**Current proposal:** Highlighted as key principle at top  
**Recommendation:** Keep it prominent. This is the conceptual anchor for the entire section and may appeal to leaders who otherwise fear this is a performance workshop.

## Additional Notes

The section should feel like an **invitation** rather than a **qualification exam**. Every entry point should say "yes, you belong here" while being honest about what the workshop is and isn't.

If any visitor category feels forced or generic, it should be removed or reframed. The goal is authentic recognition, not comprehensive coverage.
